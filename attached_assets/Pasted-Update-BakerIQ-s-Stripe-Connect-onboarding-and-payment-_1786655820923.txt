Update BakerIQ’s Stripe Connect onboarding and payment settings experience so we make better use of the payment methods already available through Stripe.

## Goal

Right now bakers are asked to connect Stripe, but we are not clearly explaining the benefit of doing so.

We want the onboarding to communicate that connecting BakerIQ Payments through Stripe gives the baker access to multiple customer payment options, not just traditional credit/debit card processing.

We also want a Payment Methods settings page after Stripe onboarding where bakers can see and optionally customize which Stripe-supported methods they want to offer customers.

IMPORTANT: Preserve our existing Stripe Connect payment architecture, application-fee logic, direct-charge model, subscription plans, transaction fees, webhook handling, payout routing, connected-account ownership, and connected-account liability structure. Do not change the current funds flow.

## Part 1 — Update the Stripe onboarding step

Find the existing onboarding step that asks the baker to connect Stripe.

Do not remove or replace the Stripe Connect onboarding flow.

Update the presentation and copy so the focus is on what the baker receives by connecting.

Use language similar to:

### Set Up BakerIQ Payments

**Connect once. Give your customers more ways to pay.**

BakerIQ Payments is powered by Stripe and can give eligible customers multiple ways to pay, including options such as:

* Credit & debit cards
* Cash App Pay
* Apple Pay
* Link
* Other eligible payment methods available through Stripe

Payment availability varies based on the baker's Stripe account, location, customer device, transaction eligibility, and Stripe requirements.

Primary CTA:

**Set Up BakerIQ Payments**

Below or near the CTA, include smaller trust/supporting copy such as:

**Secure payments powered by Stripe. Your customers pay you directly, and BakerIQ automatically records the transaction and applicable platform fee.**

Do not hide Stripe completely. We should clearly disclose that payments are powered by Stripe, but BakerIQ Payments should be the main user-facing product language.

## Part 2 — Show the benefit visually before they connect

On the Stripe onboarding step, add a clean visual section showing examples of payment types the baker may be able to offer after connecting.

Use a heading such as:

### More Ways for Your Customers to Pay

or:

### Payment Options May Include

Show a small curated set of recognizable payment options.

Our current Stripe platform configuration shows methods including:

* Cards
* Apple Pay
* Cash App Pay
* Link
* Amazon Pay
* Affirm
* Afterpay / Clearpay
* Klarna

Do not imply that every baker or every customer is guaranteed access to every method.

Do not display all international payment methods from our Stripe account.

Prioritize methods that are relevant to U.S. bakers.

The point of this section is to make the baker understand:

**Connecting Stripe is what unlocks these payment options inside BakerIQ.**

## Part 3 — After successful Stripe Connect onboarding

Once Stripe reports that the connected account has successfully completed the required onboarding and its payment capabilities are available, continue onboarding to a new step:

### Choose How Customers Can Pay

Subtitle:

**Your payment setup is ready. These are the payment options currently available through your BakerIQ Payments account.**

Also include supporting text such as:

**You can use the recommended defaults or customize them anytime.**

This step should retrieve the connected account's actual Stripe payment-method configuration, capabilities, and eligibility.

Only display methods that Stripe reports as available or configurable for that specific connected account.

Do not use a completely static list.

Example display:

Cards — On
Cash App Pay — On
Apple Pay — On
Link — On
Affirm — Off
Afterpay — Off
Klarna — Off

If Stripe requires additional information or approval for a payment method, show an appropriate status such as:

**Additional information required**

or:

**Not currently available**

Do not fake eligibility.

## Part 4 — Keep this new step low-friction

The new **Choose How Customers Can Pay** step must NOT become another hard onboarding requirement.

Its purpose is to show the baker the value they just unlocked and give them optional control.

Use sensible Stripe defaults based on the payment methods already enabled and available for that connected account.

When the baker reaches this step:

* Show eligible payment methods already enabled by default.
* Clearly indicate that these payment options became available because they connected BakerIQ Payments.
* Allow the baker to turn optional methods on or off.
* Do not require them to make any changes before continuing.
* Do not require them to manually select at least one method if Stripe already has valid defaults.
* Do not block onboarding completion because they skipped customization.
* Do not force them to understand every payment method before moving forward.

Primary CTA:

**Looks Good — Continue**

Secondary action:

**Customize Payment Methods**

If the baker chooses not to customize anything, preserve Stripe's existing/default payment-method configuration and continue onboarding normally.

The baker should always be able to change these settings later.

This step should feel like:

**“Look what you just unlocked.”**

Not:

**“Here is another form you need to complete.”**

## Part 5 — Permanent Payment Methods settings page

Create or update a section under:

**Settings → Payments → Payment Methods**

The baker should be able to return here later and manage which eligible Stripe payment methods are offered to customers.

Use Stripe's supported Payment Method Configurations, connected-account capabilities, or appropriate current APIs.

Investigate whether Stripe Connect's embedded Payment Method Settings component is available and production-ready for our current Stripe account and integration.

If it is appropriate and fully available, it may be used.

If it is unavailable, preview-only, restricted, or inappropriate for our setup, create a native BakerIQ UI backed by Stripe's supported APIs instead.

Do not depend on experimental functionality without verifying it first.

## Part 6 — Payment method categories

For U.S. BakerIQ users, keep the settings simple.

Consider grouping available methods into categories such as:

### Everyday Payments

* Cards
* Apple Pay
* Cash App Pay
* Link

### Flexible Payment Options

* Affirm
* Afterpay / Clearpay
* Klarna

Only display a method if it is actually available/configurable for that connected account.

Do not clutter the UI with country-specific methods that are irrelevant to that baker.

## Part 7 — Checkout behavior

Review BakerIQ's existing customer invoice and deposit checkout.

Make sure the checkout uses Stripe's dynamic payment-method behavior where appropriate so eligible methods can appear based on:

* the connected baker's enabled methods
* the customer's device
* customer location
* browser support
* payment amount
* currency
* Stripe eligibility
* other Stripe requirements

Do not hardcode customer-facing payment buttons for methods Stripe will not actually offer for that transaction.

The customer's payment flow must remain inside BakerIQ's existing Stripe Connect architecture.

## Part 8 — Preserve BakerIQ's existing business and liability model

Do not change:

* direct-charge architecture
* connected-account merchant responsibility
* BakerIQ application/platform fees
* Free / Basic / Pro transaction-fee percentages
* payout routing
* Stripe Connect account ownership
* refunds responsibility
* disputes/chargebacks responsibility
* connected-account negative balance responsibility
* existing webhook processing
* payment reconciliation
* subscription billing

BakerIQ should continue receiving its existing platform/application fee exactly as it does now.

The baker should remain responsible for their own customer transactions, refunds, disputes, products, and services under the existing Stripe Connect structure.

Do not accidentally convert this into a destination-charge or separate-charges-and-transfers architecture.

## Part 9 — Existing users

Do not limit this feature to new accounts.

Existing bakers who already completed Stripe Connect should get access to:

**Settings → Payments → Payment Methods**

Existing bakers who have not connected Stripe should see the improved BakerIQ Payments messaging wherever we currently encourage them to connect payments.

If appropriate, show a small informational banner to existing connected bakers such as:

**You may have more ways to get paid. Review your available payment methods.**

Do not make this disruptive or mandatory.

Include where appropriate:

* connected account ID
* plan tier

## Part 11 — Do not alter the current onboarding experiment unnecessarily

We are currently monitoring onboarding and Stripe activation behavior.

Do not redesign unrelated onboarding steps.

Do not move major existing steps unless technically required.

The goal is specifically to improve how the Stripe/BakerIQ Payments step communicates value and to add optional payment-method customization.

Keep the rest of onboarding stable.

## Part 12 — Error handling and edge cases

Handle the following cleanly:

* Stripe onboarding incomplete
* connected account restricted
* capabilities pending
* payment method unavailable
* payment method requiring additional verification
* API error loading payment methods
* baker returning from Stripe before onboarding is complete
* baker already connected before this feature existed

Do not leave users on broken or empty screens.

If payment-method configuration cannot be retrieved, allow the baker to continue onboarding and show a clear message that payment settings can be reviewed later.

Payment-method customization should never unnecessarily block the baker from using BakerIQ.

## Part 13 — Before coding

Before making changes, inspect the current:

* Stripe Connect account type/configuration
* direct-charge implementation
* application-fee implementation
* Checkout / Payment Element implementation
* current payment-method configuration
* account capability handling
* Stripe onboarding completion logic
* payment webhooks
* refunds/disputes handling
* Payments settings page
* onboarding analytics

Then summarize what currently exists and identify exactly which:

* frontend components
* backend routes
* Stripe API calls
* database fields
* analytics events
* onboarding screens

will need to be modified.

Do not change code until you understand the existing payment architecture.
